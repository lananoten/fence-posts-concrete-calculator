Fence Posts That Stay Straight

Most leaning fences do not fail at the rail. They fail at the hole: too shallow, too narrow, or set in wet soil that heaves. Get the hole right and the fence stays plumb.

Three Rules for a Post That Does Not Lean

  • Depth beats width: a common rule is one-third of the post above ground buried, with a minimum that suits your frost line. Shallow posts lever out in wind.
  • Below frost where it freezes: in cold climates, a post base above the frost line will heave. Bell the bottom or use a wider base to resist uplift.
  • Drain the base: a post sitting in a concrete cup that holds water rots faster. Slope the concrete crown away from the post and keep soil off the wood.

A Fence Run, Worked Through

Eight holes, each 12 inches across and 30 inches deep: radius 6 inches is 0.5 feet, depth 2.5 feet, so each hole is 3.1416 × 0.5² × 2.5 = 1.96 cubic feet. Eight holes give 15.71 cubic feet, or 0.58 cubic yards neat. With a 10% buffer, plan for 0.64 yards — about 29 eighty-pound bags. That is a comfortable bag job, and it is why many fence builders mix on site rather than ordering a truck for a short run.

Gate posts are the exception: they carry a swinging load, so go deeper and wider than line posts, and brace them before the concrete sets.

Fence Post Questions

Should I use concrete or gravel for fence posts?

Concrete gives the strongest, most wind-resistant set, especially for gates and corners. Gravel drains better and suits light fences in dry ground, but posts can shift more over time.

How deep should a fence post hole be?

As a starting point, bury about one-third of the post, and always get below the local frost depth in cold climates. Check local practice for your soil and fence height.

Can I set posts in dry-mix concrete?

Dry-mix works for light line posts in the right conditions, but for gates, corners, and wet ground, properly mixed concrete gives a more reliable result.

How long before I can hang a gate?

Wait until the concrete has gained enough strength — typically a few days for a gate post, longer in cold weather. Hanging a heavy gate too early is a classic way to pull a fresh post out of plumb.
